Antimony is an open-source platform for designing, deploying and operating network labs. It builds on [containerlab](https://containerlab.dev): you describe a network as a containerlab topology, and Antimony turns it into running routers, switches and hosts, either on a single Docker host or on a Kubernetes cluster with [clabernetes](https://containerlab.dev/manual/clabernetes/).

Everything happens in the browser:

- **Design** topologies in a graph editor or in YAML, side by side.
- **Deploy** labs right away or schedule them, one at a time or for a whole class.
- **Operate** them: open terminals on nodes, follow logs, watch CPU, memory and interface traffic, and stream packets into Wireshark.
- **Share** them: organise topologies in collections and control who can see, change and deploy them through your organisation's login.

Antimony was started as a bachelor's thesis at the [OST Eastern Switzerland University of Applied Sciences](https://www.ost.ch) and is maintained by its [Institute for Networking](https://www.ost.ch/en/research-and-consulting-services/computer-science/ins-institute-for-network-and-security). It's used to teach networking: students get their own labs, and instructors prepare topologies once and deploy them for everyone.
